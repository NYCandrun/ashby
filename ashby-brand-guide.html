Brand guidelines.

How Ashby looks, reads, and behaves. Every color, typeface, and measurement in this guide comes from the live site at ashbyhq.com, using Ashby's own token names.

Compiled September 2026 from ashbyhq.com production CSS and thirty page captures.

Wordmark

One word, drawn once.

The Ashby logo is the word "Ashby" in a bold cut of Signifier, a high-contrast serif from Klim Type Foundry. It ships as an outlined SVG asset, not as typeset text, and every wordmark in this guide is that exact file, pulled from ashbyhq.com. The paths fill with currentColor, so one asset covers every color treatment.

It is the only place a serif carries the brand alone. Everything around it, from navigation to headlines to body copy, is set in TT Norms Pro, so the serif wordmark reads as a signature against a sans-serif page. The wordmark is always sentence case with a capital A, never paired with a symbol or icon in marketing contexts.

Use the asset, not the font

The logo's cut is heavier than the Signifier Regular webfont the site ships, so typing "Ashby" in the available font produces a wordmark that is visibly too light. Always place the SVG.

Clear space

Keep clear space around the wordmark equal to the height of the lowercase letters. At navigation size the wordmark sits at roughly 24px tall; it should never render below 20px, where the serif's thin strokes start to break up.

Color

Indigo on quiet neutrals.

Ashby's pages are almost entirely white and cool near-white. Indigo does the work: it colors the wordmark, every primary button, links, and section eyebrows. Semantic colors appear only inside product UI mockups, and the four accent hues appear only in charts and illustration. The result is a page that reads calm, with color signaling exactly one thing, which is where to act.

The current site runs on a brighter indigo than the legacy primary token. Buttons carry a subtle vertical gradient that starts at a lighter indigo and settles into the base.

Typography

Two typefaces with strict jobs.

TT Norms Pro, a geometric sans from TypeType, sets everything: headlines, navigation, body, buttons, and captions. It ships as a variable font covering weights 100 through 900, though the brand uses only 400, 500, 600, and 700. Signifier, from Klim Type Foundry, ships in regular and italic only, and appears as editorial display on story-style pages; a heavier cut of it forms the wordmark.

Wide-tracked labels

Trusted by top teams

Product

The one sanctioned use of all caps: small labels at roughly 12px, semibold, with letter-spacing opened to 0.1em, in Neutral 500 or 600. They caption logo walls and head footer columns. Headlines are never set in caps.

Components

Soft, rounded, and quiet until you need them.

Ashby's interface language is white surfaces on pale gray pages, 1px borders, a 10px base radius, and rounded rectangles or pills for anything tappable. Primary actions are the only saturated elements on the page.

Buttons

Primary buttons carry a subtle vertical indigo gradient, sampled at #6355DB fading to #4C40D0, and settle to flat indigo on hover over 0.25s ease-in-out. Secondary buttons are white with a neutral border. Text links sit in interactive indigo, and marketing links often end with an arrow. Labels stay short and verb-first. The flagship CTA is written "Get in Touch" in title case wherever it appears; most other buttons use sentence case, and the site tolerates both.

Layout

A 1200px stage with generous air.

Content lives in a 1200px maximum container with 24px side gutters. Sections are centered, hero copy is center-aligned, and feature sections alternate between text-left and text-right around large product imagery. Big rounded panels, often 16 to 24px radius, group entire sections against the page background.

Motion

Two durations and two curves cover the whole site: 0.25s standard and 0.35s long, with ease-in-out as the default and a gentle natural curve, cubic-bezier(0.36, 0.12, 0.44, 1.04), for larger movements. Motion answers hover and click; nothing animates on its own.

Imagery

Product imagery is the brand's illustration style. Real interface fragments, such as scheduling chips, candidate cards, and AI assistant buttons, float at slight angles over soft lavender panels, with a cursor often mid-click. Screenshots are cropped into rounded panels rather than shown as full windows. A second standing style, glassy translucent 3D isometric renders like stacked frosted layers with floating icon chips, in lavender with small hits of orange, green, and navy, illustrates pricing plans and older product pages. There is no abstract decoration and no stock photography in product contexts; people appear only in customer stories and event pages.

Logo wall

The homepage sets customer logos in dark monochrome, two rows of about ten on the light page, captioned with a small gray label. Older product pages still show logos in their original brand colors. For new work, follow the homepage: monochrome.

Footer

The footer stays light: page background, a hairline top border, the Signifier wordmark in indigo at the left with the copyright line and compliance badges below it, then link columns headed by the wide-tracked caps labels. Combined items use a middle dot, as in "Ashby All-in-one • Startups".

Two page generations

The site currently mixes two template generations. The homepage and newest pages use sentence-case headlines, indigo eyebrows, monochrome logos, and floating-UI imagery. Older pages, such as Enterprise and Growth, use title-case section headings, dark eyebrows, and color logos. When reproducing the brand, follow the homepage generation; treat the older patterns as legacy, except the 3D isometric illustration style, which remains current on pricing.

Voice

Declarative sentences that end with a period.

Ashby's headlines are short, confident statements about the product. Heroes frequently end in a full stop, which makes them read as verdicts rather than slogans. Copy stays concrete about what the product does, names its categories plainly (ATS, CRM, Scheduling, Analytics), and lets customer logos and data carry the proof.

Do

  • Lead with the product category and a plain claim.
  • Use sentence case for headlines; keep "Get in Touch" and nav items in title case.
  • End hero statements with a period.
  • Name features by what they do: AI Notetaker, Advanced Scheduling.
  • Keep button labels to two or three words.

Don't

  • Write in exclamation points or hype adjectives.
  • Set headlines in all caps; caps belong only to small wide-tracked labels.
  • Use the serif in product marketing headlines; it belongs to the wordmark and editorial pages.
  • Add color that doesn't signal an action or a state.
  • Bury the claim in a metaphor.
